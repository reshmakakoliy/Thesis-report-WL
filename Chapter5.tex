\section{Summary of the Study}
\label{sec:5.2}

Floods and water-logging are a recurring threat in Bangladesh, and the
stations along its river network behave very differently from one another.
Earlier forecasting studies tend to use a narrow set of variables, to assume
that data can be pooled in one place, and to say little about why a model
predicts what it does. This thesis addressed these gaps by proposing
CFL-EDLSTM, a Clustered Federated Learning framework built on an
encoder--decoder LSTM, for multi-horizon water-level forecasting.

Using observations from 25 monitoring stations within the Brahmaputra River
system, with water level as the target and discharge, rainfall, temperature and
evaporation as inputs, the stations were grouped into four clusters: Upstream,
Mid-river, Downstream and Urban. Each cluster acted as a federated client,
trained locally, and contributed to a weighted federated-averaging step, while
keeping a model adapted to its own stations. Forecasts were produced at seven
horizons from 3~hours to 15~days and compared with four baselines (Vanilla
LSTM, GRU, XGBoost and TCN). The trained models were then examined with SHAP,
and the forecasts were made available through a web interface that also
compares them with FFWC forecasts.

\section{Major Findings}
\label{sec:5.3}

The results of Chapter~4 lead to five main findings.

First, CFL-EDLSTM was the most accurate model overall, with an MAE of
0.321~m, an RMSE of 0.521~m and an NSE of 0.998. Its MAE was about 35\% lower
than that of the best neural baseline (Vanilla LSTM) and about 40\% lower than
that of XGBoost (Section~\ref{sec:4.5}).

Second, the best model depends on the horizon. XGBoost was more accurate up to
a one-day lead, but its error grew roughly sixteen-fold by 15~days, against
about threefold for CFL-EDLSTM, which was the most accurate model from three
days onward (15-day MAE of 0.436~m against 1.149~m for XGBoost). The longer
horizons are where an early warning is most useful (Sections~\ref{sec:4.6}
and~\ref{sec:4.8}).

Third, accuracy was fairly even across the network but not uniform. MAE ranged
from 0.252~m in the Urban cluster to 0.394~m in the Mid-river cluster, and
CFL-EDLSTM had the lowest MAE at 24 of the 25 stations. The exception was
SW291.5R, the highest-level station, where three other models did better
(Sections~\ref{sec:4.7} and~\ref{sec:4.10}).

Fourth, SHAP showed that the models relied most on seasonal timing (day of
year) and temperature, with rainfall accumulations contributing less and
discharge features not appearing among the top 20, and that attribution was
concentrated in the last few days of the 30-day input window
(Section~\ref{sec:shap_results}). This describes what the models learned, not
what drives the rivers.

Fifth, in a short check against FFWC forecasts issued in October 2026,
CFL-EDLSTM had the lower MAE for lead days 1 to 7 against FFWC's 10-day
forecast and for days 1 to 3 against its 5-day forecast, while FFWC was ahead at
the longest leads. The period was short and calm, so this is an encouraging
first check and not a verdict (Section~\ref{sec:ffwc_comparison}).

Compared with earlier work, the gain over a plain LSTM is of a similar size to
that reported for the regional benchmark of Ruma et al.\ \cite{Ruma2023},
within the limits of an indirect comparison (Section~\ref{sec:4.13}).

\section{Research Contributions}
\label{sec:5.5}

The thesis makes four contributions. It shows that stations in the Brahmaputra
River system can be grouped by hydrological behavior into four clusters that
work as federated clients without pooling raw data across them. It offers
CFL-EDLSTM, a clustered federated encoder--decoder LSTM that, in these
experiments, forecasts water level more accurately than four established
baselines from three days to 15~days ahead. It provides a SHAP-based picture of
which hydrometeorological inputs the models rely on. And it delivers a working
web interface that places the forecasts beside FFWC's, so that the framework can
be inspected by someone other than its authors. To our knowledge, no earlier
study had brought these elements together for Bangladesh.

\section{Limitations of the Study}
\label{sec:5.6}

The findings should be read with the following limits in mind.

\paragraph{Data.}
The records contain unexpected gaps, and a few stations report only during the
monsoon. Gaps were filled, so filled values are estimates and not observations.
Discharge is missing entirely at ten of the 25 stations, and rainfall is zero on
about three quarters of records. Data from the upstream river network in India
were not available, so the models cannot see conditions beyond the border
stations.

\paragraph{Model and evaluation.}
The federation was simulated on a single machine, each model was trained once
without a fixed seed, and no systematic hyperparameter search was carried out.
No ablation separates the contributions of clustering, federation and the
encoder--decoder, so we describe the combined design as effective without
crediting any one part. The clusters were fixed in advance from domain
knowledge and not learned. Performance was uneven at SW291.5R, and XGBoost
remained better up to about one day.

\paragraph{Interpretation and comparison.}
SHAP values describe the models and not causal effects, and correlated inputs
share credit. The SHAP sample was small. The FFWC comparison covers a short,
post-monsoon period of hindcasts with few matched points, and the comparison with
earlier studies is indirect because datasets, periods and protocols differ. The
web interface is a research prototype and not an operational warning system.

\section{Future Research Directions}
\label{sec:5.7}

Several directions follow directly from these limits.

The most immediate is to strengthen the evidence. Ablation runs would separate
the effects of clustering, federation and the encoder--decoder, with a global
encoder--decoder, a clustered model without the encoder--decoder and local-only
models as references, and repeated runs with different seeds would show how
stable the results are. A systematic hyperparameter search and an evaluation
over a full flood season, with a longer FFWC record, would also make the
conclusions firmer.

A second direction is to improve the inputs and the clustering. Upstream data
from across the border, satellite or forecast rainfall and more complete
discharge records could help the Upstream and Mid-river clusters in particular.
Clusters learned from the data, instead of fixed in advance, could be compared
with the hydrological grouping used here.

A third direction is to extend the method and its use. Graph-based and
attention-based models, which we examined but did not include, could be combined
with clustered federation, and probabilistic forecasts would give users an
uncertainty range. On the deployment side, a truly distributed federated setup,
a live data feed for the interface and a repeat of the SHAP analysis with a
larger sample would move the work toward practical use.

\section{Final Conclusion}
\label{sec:5.8}

This thesis asked whether a clustered federated encoder--decoder LSTM could
forecast water level across hydrologically different stations in Bangladesh
without pooling their raw data. Within the limits described above, the answer
is yes. CFL-EDLSTM gave the lowest overall error of the five models, held its
accuracy far better than XGBoost as the lead time grew, and was the most
accurate model from three days to 15~days, the horizons that matter most for
early warning. SHAP and a web interface that sits beside FFWC's forecasts make
those predictions easier to inspect and trust. The results are an encouraging
step and not a finished system, and the work that remains is mostly about
testing these ideas more thoroughly and on live data.
