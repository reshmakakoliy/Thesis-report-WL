\section{Summary of the Study}
\label{sec:5.2}

Floods and water-logging are a recurring threat in Bangladesh, and the
monitoring stations along its river network behave very differently from one
another. Earlier forecasting studies tend to use a narrow set of variables,
assume that data can be pooled in one place, and offer little insight into why
a model makes the predictions it does. This thesis addressed these gaps by
proposing CFL-EDLSTM, a Clustered Federated Learning framework built on an
encoder--decoder LSTM, for multi-horizon water-level forecasting.

The study used observations from 25 monitoring stations within the Brahmaputra
River system, with water level as the target and discharge, rainfall,
temperature and evaporation as inputs. The stations were grouped into four
clusters: Upstream, Mid-river, Downstream and Urban. Each cluster acted as a
federated client that trained locally and contributed to a weighted federated
averaging step, while retaining a model adapted to its own stations. Forecasts
were produced at seven horizons from 3~hours to 15~days and compared with four
baselines: Vanilla LSTM, GRU, XGBoost and TCN. Three of these baselines were
also trained in clustered federated form, GRU and TCN under the same scheme and
XGBoost under a prediction-level counterpart. The trained models were then
examined with SHAP, and the forecasts were made available through a web
interface that also compares them with FFWC's operational forecasts.

\section{Major Findings}
\label{sec:5.3}

The results presented in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} lead to five main findings.

First, CFL-EDLSTM was the most accurate model overall, with an MAE of 0.321~m,
an RMSE of 0.521~m and an NSE of 0.998. Its MAE was about 35\% lower than that
of the best neural baseline (Vanilla LSTM) and about 40\% lower than that of
XGBoost (Section~\ref{sec:4.5}). When GRU, TCN and XGBoost were also trained in
clustered federated form, CFL-EDLSTM still had the lowest MAE at every horizon,
which supports the choice of the encoder--decoder as the model to federate
(Section~\ref{sec:4.5.1}).

Second, the best model depends on the forecast horizon. XGBoost was more
accurate up to a one-day lead, but its error grew roughly sixteen-fold by
15~days, compared with about threefold for CFL-EDLSTM. CFL-EDLSTM was the most
accurate model from three days onward, with a 15-day MAE of 0.436~m against
1.149~m for XGBoost. These longer horizons are where early warning is most
valuable (Sections~\ref{sec:4.6} and~\ref{sec:4.8}).

Third, accuracy was fairly consistent across the network but not uniform. MAE
ranged from 0.252~m in the Urban cluster to 0.394~m in the Mid-river cluster,
and CFL-EDLSTM had the lowest MAE at 24 of the 25 stations. The exception was
SW291.5R, the highest-level station, where three other models achieved a lower
MAE (Sections~\ref{sec:4.7} and~\ref{sec:4.10}).

Fourth, with past water level excluded from the reported attributions, SHAP
showed that the models relied most on seasonal timing (day of year) and
temperature, with rainfall accumulations contributing less and no discharge
feature among the top 20. Attribution was concentrated in the last few days of
the 30-day input window (Section~\ref{sec:4.9}). These results describe what the
models learned, not what drives the rivers.

Fifth, in a short comparison with FFWC forecasts issued in October 2026,
CFL-EDLSTM had the lower MAE for lead days 1 to 7 against FFWC's 10-day forecast
and for days 1 to 3 against its 5-day forecast, while FFWC was more accurate at
the longest lead times. Because the period was short and hydrologically calm,
this is an encouraging first check rather than a conclusive result
(Section~\ref{sec:4.11}).

Compared with earlier work, the improvement over a plain LSTM is of a similar
size to that reported for the regional benchmark of Ruma et al.\
\cite{Ruma2023}, within the limits of an indirect comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:4.13}).

\section{Research Contributions}
\label{sec:5.5}

This thesis makes five contributions. First, it shows that stations in the
Brahmaputra River system can be grouped by hydrological behavior into four
clusters that function as federated clients without pooling raw data across
them. Second, it proposes CFL-EDLSTM, a clustered federated encoder--decoder
LSTM for multivariate, multi-horizon water-level forecasting. Third, it compares
this model with four established baselines, trained centrally and, for three of
them, in clustered federated form, across clusters and seven horizons; in these
experiments, CFL-EDLSTM had the lowest MAE of all models from three to 15~days
ahead. Fourth, it provides a SHAP-based account of the hydrometeorological
inputs on which the models rely. Fifth, it delivers a working web interface
that places the forecasts alongside FFWC's operational forecasts, so that the
framework can be examined by users other than its authors. To the best of our
knowledge, no earlier study has brought these elements together for Bangladesh.

\section{Limitations of the Study}
\label{sec:5.6}

The findings should be interpreted in light of the following limitations.

\paragraph{Data.}
The records contain unexpected gaps, and a few stations report only during the
monsoon. Because gaps were filled, the filled values are estimates rather than
observations. Discharge is missing entirely at ten of the 25 stations, and
rainfall is zero in about three quarters of the records. Data from the upstream
river network in India were not available, so the models cannot see conditions
beyond the border stations.

\paragraph{Model and evaluation.}
The federation was simulated on a single machine, each model was trained once
without a fixed random seed, and no systematic hyperparameter search was
carried out. Training the baselines in clustered federated form shows that the
encoder--decoder was the most accurate architecture, but no non-federated or
single global encoder--decoder was trained, so the effects of clustering and
federation on their own are not isolated. The centrally trained baselines also
use fewer input features than CFL-EDLSTM. The clusters were fixed in advance
from domain knowledge rather than learned from the data. Performance was weaker
at SW291.5R, and XGBoost remained more accurate up to about one day ahead.

\paragraph{Interpretation and comparison.}
SHAP values describe the behavior of the models, not causal effects, and
correlated inputs share credit. The SHAP sample was also small. The FFWC
comparison covers a short post-monsoon period of hindcasts with few matched
points, and the comparison with earlier studies is indirect because the
datasets, periods and protocols differ. Finally, the web interface is a
research prototype, not an operational warning system.

\section{Future Research Directions}
\label{sec:5.7}

Several directions for future work follow directly from these limitations.

The most immediate is to strengthen the evidence. Training a global
encoder--decoder on all stations, together with local-only models, would
isolate the effect of clustering and federation, and repeated runs with
different seeds would show how stable the results are. A systematic
hyperparameter search and an evaluation over a full flood season, with a longer
FFWC record, would further strengthen the conclusions.

A second direction is to improve the inputs and the clustering. Upstream data
from across the border, satellite or forecast rainfall and more complete
discharge records could benefit the Upstream and Mid-river clusters in
particular. Clusters learned from the data, rather than fixed in advance, could
also be compared with the hydrological grouping used here.

A third direction is to extend the method and its application. Graph-based and
attention-based models, which were considered but not included
(Section~\ref{sec:3.13}), could be combined with clustered federated training,
and probabilistic forecasts would give users an uncertainty range. On the
deployment side, a genuinely distributed federated setup, a live data feed for
the interface and a repeated SHAP analysis with a larger sample would move the
work closer to practical use.

\section{Final Conclusion}
\label{sec:5.8}

This thesis asked whether a clustered federated encoder--decoder LSTM could
forecast water level across hydrologically different stations in Bangladesh
without pooling their raw data. Within the limitations described above, the
answer is yes. CFL-EDLSTM achieved the lowest overall error of the models
compared, maintained its accuracy far better than XGBoost as the lead time
increased, and was the most accurate model from three to 15~days ahead, the
horizons that matter most for early warning. SHAP and a web interface that
places the forecasts alongside FFWC's operational forecasts make these
predictions easier to inspect and trust. The results represent an encouraging
step rather than a finished system, and the remaining work lies mainly in
testing these ideas more thoroughly and on live data.
